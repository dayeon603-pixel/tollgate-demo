Signed proof of which sanctions list you checked, and when.

For stablecoin issuers and exchanges.

What a receipt contains

  • The decision: allow or block, and the list entry it matched
  • The address and amount that were checked
  • A fingerprint of the exact list used
  • The time of the check
  • A digital signature over the decision
  • A link to the receipt before it

How it runs

  • You run Tollgate on your own machine.
  • It downloads OFAC's sanctions list and compares addresses locally.
  • The addresses you check stay on your machine.
  • Your signing key is created there too.
  • If you seal receipts, only their hashes go out, to public timestamp services.
  • Anyone with a receipt can check it offline, without contacting us.

What this check does and doesn't prove

What it checks

  • The signature matches the signed decision.
  • The decision was signed by the sample key.
  • The payment details match the signed decision.
  • Each receipt's fingerprint matches its contents.
  • Each receipt links to the one before it.

The check runs in this browser and sends nothing anywhere. It shows these receipts are unchanged since they were signed. It does not re-run the screening.

What it doesn't do

  • Match names or use other lists. It matches exact addresses on OFAC's list only.
  • Move, freeze or block funds. Your system acts on the decision.
  • Make a legal determination.
  • Show that the list itself was complete or current.
  • Catch every removal on its own. Someone with the files could remove a receipt and relink the rest, and this check would still pass. A seal shows that, and only for receipts up to the last seal.
  • Notice a dropped last receipt. Without a seal, a file missing its last receipt still passes. To see it happen, choose "Drop the last receipt" next to Simulate tampering.

About this sample

Made by Tollgate's signing engine. The payments are made up and never happened. The blocked addresses are real entries on a public copy of OFAC's list from August 2023. The signing key is a published demo key that anyone can use, so this sample shows how the check works and says nothing about who signed.
